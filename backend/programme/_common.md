# NUS Common Academic Terms & University-Wide Requirements

This file covers conventions and requirements that are shared across multiple
NUS undergraduate programmes, so they don't need to be repeated (or looked
up) separately for every major. Programme-specific documents (`CS.md`,
`BBA.md`, `BAIS.md`, `BZA.md`, `InfoSec.md`, ...) describe how these general
rules are instantiated for that particular major - if a specific programme
doc contradicts something here, the programme doc is more authoritative for
that programme.

## Glossary

- **MC (Modular Credit)**: the unit used to measure course workload and
  progress towards a degree. Most courses are worth 4 MCs; some are worth 1,
  2, or 3. "Units" in programme requirement documents means MCs.
- **CAP (Cumulative Average Point)**: NUS's GPA-equivalent, on a 5.0 scale,
  computed from graded (non-S/U'd) courses.
- **S/U (Satisfactory/Unsatisfactory)**: a grading option a student can apply
  to an eligible course instead of receiving a letter grade. A pass ("S")
  still earns the course's MCs towards graduation but does **not** affect
  CAP; a fail ("U") earns no MCs and also does not affect CAP. Not every
  course is S/U-able - core/foundation courses in a student's own major
  usually aren't - and the number of courses a student may S/U, and any
  extra allowances (e.g. a more generous S/U quota in Year 1), vary by
  faculty and cohort. Do not assume a specific numeric cap unless it's
  stated in the specific programme's own document.
- **ULR (University Level Requirements)** / **GE (General Education)**: the
  university-wide breadth requirement all undergraduates must satisfy,
  independent of major - see "University Level Requirements" below. Often
  used interchangeably with "GE" in NUS documents.
- **Common Curriculum**: the portion of a degree's requirements that isn't
  specific to the student's major - typically ULR/GE courses plus other
  university- or faculty-wide requirements (e.g. Computing Ethics for School
  of Computing students, or Business Environment courses for BIZ students).
- **Programme Requirements** (also called **Major Requirements**): the
  courses specific to the student's declared major.
- **Unrestricted Electives (UE)**: units that can be fulfilled by almost any
  NUS course, used for exploration, a minor, second major, or double degree.
- **Honours classification**: NUS Bachelor's degrees are typically awarded
  with Honours (Highest Distinction / Distinction / Merit / and plain
  Honours), which usually requires completing a dissertation, capstone, or
  equivalent high-level project - the specific course (e.g. CP4101, BT4101)
  and eligibility rules (e.g. a minimum CAP) vary by programme.
- **Course level numbering**: the first digit of a course code roughly
  indicates year/difficulty level - 1000 (introductory, typically Year 1),
  2000-3000 (intermediate, Year 2-3), 4000 (advanced/capstone, typically
  Year 4), 5000+ (graduate-level, occasionally taken by strong
  undergraduates or cross-listed as an elective).
- **NOC (NUS Overseas Colleges)**: an entrepreneurship-focused overseas
  internship programme; students who complete it can often use specific NOC
  courses (e.g. ETP3202L, ETP3203L, ETP3206L) to satisfy part of their
  Industry Experience Requirement, dissertation, or electives - the exact
  mapping is programme-specific.
- **Industry Experience Requirement (IER)**: a requirement common to several
  Bachelor of Computing programmes (see below) to complete an internship of
  a minimum duration, sometimes replaceable by a dissertation for
  high-CAP students.

## Grading Scale

NUS uses letter grades, each mapped to a grade point used to compute CAP:

| Letter Grade | Grade Point |
|---|---|
| A+ / A | 5.0 |
| A- | 4.5 |
| B+ | 4.0 |
| B | 3.5 |
| B- | 3.0 |
| C+ | 2.5 |
| C | 2.0 |
| D+ | 1.5 |
| D | 1.0 |
| F | 0.0 |

A course graded S/U does not receive a letter grade or grade point and is
excluded from CAP entirely (see S/U in the Glossary above).

## Module Load & Overloading

The typical full-time workload is **20 units (MCs) per semester**. Students
may take on a heavier workload (commonly referred to as 'overloading'), up to a maximum of **40 units
in a single semester** - but that ceiling isn't available to everyone by
default. The actual maximum a given student may register for is calculated
individually based on their past academic performance (CAP), and a student
wanting to exceed their calculated limit generally needs to submit a
separate application (e.g. via CourseReg) for approval, rather than being
able to just register for the extra units directly.

## University Level Requirements (ULR) / General Education

Every NUS undergraduate, regardless of major, must complete one course from
each of six GE pillars (24 units total, 4 units/course):

- Data Literacy (GEA)
- Digital Literacy (GEI)
- Critique and Expression (GEX)
- Cultures and Connections (GEC)
- Singapore Studies (GESS)
- Communities and Engagement (GEN)

Many majors satisfy one or two of these pillars with a specific
programme-required course instead of a generic GE-coded course - e.g.
Computer Science students satisfy Digital Literacy with CS1101S rather than
a GEI-coded course, and Business Artificial Intelligence Systems students
satisfy Data Literacy with BT1101. This is normal; it doesn't mean the
pillar was skipped. The full course list for each pillar, and the specific
substitution rules per major, are in `GE.md` rather than here, since that
list is long and mostly only relevant when a student is actually choosing a
GE course.

## Typical Bachelor's Degree Shape (160 units)

Most Bachelor's programmes covered in this dataset require 160 units total,
split into three parts:

1. **Common Curriculum** (ULR/GE plus other shared requirements - the exact
   makeup differs by faculty, see below)
2. **Programme/Major Requirements**
3. **Unrestricted Electives**

A common rule across Bachelor programmes: **at least 80% of a major's unit
requirements must be at level 1000-4000** (i.e. not graduate-level).

Business Administration (BBA) is a structural exception - see "Business
School Common Curriculum" below - so don't assume every programme's Common
Curriculum is 40 units just because several School of Computing programmes
are.

## School of Computing (SoC) Common Curriculum

Shared by Computer Science, Business Artificial Intelligence Systems,
Business Analytics, and Information Security (and likely other
SoC-hosted programmes not yet in this dataset). Common Curriculum is 40
units, made up of:

- University Level Requirements (ULR/GE, 6 pillars): 24 units
- Computing Ethics (IS1108 Digital Ethics/AI Ethics and Data Privacy): 4 units
- Interdisciplinary & Cross-Disciplinary Education: 12 units (at least two
  Interdisciplinary (ID) courses, no more than one Cross-Disciplinary (CD)
  course)

These SoC programmes also share a common **Industry Experience Requirement**
pattern: a 6-month internship (e.g. CP3880, IS4010) or two/one 3-month
internships (e.g. CP3200, CP3202), usually 6-12 units. Students with a CAP
of 4.00 or higher after completing at least 70% (112 units) of their degree
may generally opt to replace this with a dissertation course (e.g. CP4101,
BT4101) instead, and Honours (Highest Distinction) generally requires
passing that dissertation. Exact unit counts and course codes still differ
per major - check the specific programme document.

## Business School (BIZ) Common Curriculum

The BBA programme's Common Curriculum is 52 units, structured differently
from the SoC pattern above:

- General Education (ULR/GE, 6 pillars): 24 units
- Business Environment Courses (6 courses shared across all BIZ majors): 20 units
- Cross-Disciplinary Course - Field Service Project: 8 units
- Work Experience Milestone (WEM) and Global Experience Milestone (GEM):
  non-credit-bearing milestones required for graduation

BBA students choose a primary major (e.g. Accountancy, Finance, Marketing -
see `BBA.md` for the full list of 9 majors) whose Major Curriculum
requirements are almost entirely major-specific, aside from 6 shared
Business Function courses.
